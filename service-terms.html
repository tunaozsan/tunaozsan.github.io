Proposed commercial terms

Service terms.

A clear basis for agreeing the work, the inputs, and the deliverables.

Draft for discussion — not yet effective. These proposed terms are provided for review. Services are currently offered by enquiry, with scope and contractual terms confirmed in a written agreement before work begins. This draft does not replace an existing agreement or limit mandatory legal rights.

Draft prepared October 28, 2026. Provider: Bluefin Tuna LLC.

1. Scope and written agreement

These proposed terms concern human-delivered project planning and project-controls services. A written quotation or service agreement should confirm the selected package, activity limit, required inputs, price, applicable taxes, delivery date, revisions, and any additional work. Agreed written scope takes priority over general catalogue descriptions where lawful. App Store purchases are separate and are governed by the applicable Apple and app terms.

2. Seven core packages

Current USD catalogue prices and full package descriptions are available in Services & Pricing. Each package covers one project; a schedule update covers one reporting period. The following scope is proposed for written confirmation:

  • Primavera P6 Schedule Audit: up to 150 activities; One existing schedule reviewed for logic, critical path, constraints and milestones. Deliverables: PDF findings report. Findings report only; no corrections. Proposed turnaround: 5 business days after accepted complete inputs and confirmed scope. Schedule corrections, rebuilding, resource loading and delay claims are excluded.
  • Basic Baseline — Without Resources: up to 75 activities; A focused baseline with WBS, calendars, dependencies, milestones and critical path. Deliverables: XER, PDF. 1 in-scope revision. Proposed turnaround: 5 business days after accepted complete inputs and confirmed scope. No labor MH, cost or material resource loading. No estimating or quantity takeoff.
  • Basic Baseline — With Resources: up to 75 activities; The basic baseline with client-supplied labor man-hours and cost allocation. Deliverables: XER, PDF. 1 in-scope revision. Proposed turnaround: 7 business days after accepted complete inputs and confirmed scope. Materials, equipment-hour models, budgeting, unit-rate development and quantity takeoff are excluded.
  • Detailed Baseline — Without Resources: up to 200 activities; A detailed CPM baseline, milestone list and narrative for a clearly defined project scope. Deliverables: XER, PDF, Excel. 2 in-scope revisions. Proposed turnaround: 10 business days after accepted complete inputs and confirmed scope. No labor MH, cost or material resource loading. No estimating or quantity takeoff.
  • Detailed Baseline — With Resources: up to 200 activities; The detailed CPM baseline and narrative, with client-supplied labor man-hours and cost allocation. Deliverables: XER, PDF, Excel. 2 in-scope revisions. Proposed turnaround: 12 business days after accepted complete inputs and confirmed scope. Materials, equipment-hour models, budgeting, unit-rate development and quantity takeoff are excluded.
  • Primavera P6 Schedule Update — Without Resources: up to 200 activities; One reporting period: actual dates, remaining durations, CPM and variance review, and a 3-week lookahead. Deliverables: Updated XER, PDF. Revision allowance confirmed in your written scope. Proposed turnaround: 4 business days after accepted complete inputs and confirmed scope. No labor MH, cost or material updates. Baseline creation, recovery planning and claims are excluded.
  • Primavera P6 Schedule Update — With Resources: up to 200 activities; One reporting period on an existing resource-loaded XER, including actual and remaining labor MH and costs. Deliverables: Updated XER, PDF. Revision allowance confirmed in your written scope. Proposed turnaround: 5 business days after accepted complete inputs and confirmed scope. Material updates, new resource dictionaries, initial loading and remapping are excluded.

3. Resource and material boundaries

“With Resources” means labor man-hours and cost allocation only. Physical materials, equipment-hour models, independent estimating, material takeoffs, procurement, and the creation of client budgets or unit rates are excluded unless separately agreed. Initial resource loading, material-category loading, and material updates require separate scope and quotation.

Material add-ons cover up to 50 supplied BOQ lines per independent category within the core schedule’s activity limit. Extra lines or complex mapping require a separate quote. A material update requires an existing mapping and initial loading. Resource-category limits must be confirmed in the written agreement.

4. Client inputs and responsibilities

The client supplies accurate, complete, and authorized project information: BOQ or itemized work list, descriptions, quantities, units, drawings where relevant, execution constraints, milestones, and contractual dates. Inputs are normally supplied as P6 XER, Excel, or PDF, as relevant and agreed. Other formats require confirmation.

For labor/cost loading, provide approved man-hour budgets, allocation assumptions, cost budgets, and applicable unit rates. Updates also require the current XER, approved baseline, data date, actual dates, remaining durations, and progress report; resource updates additionally require relevant man-hour and cost actuals and forecasts. Material work needs quantities, units, category mapping, and period quantities where applicable.

The client remains responsible for the accuracy, completeness, authority to share, and lawful use of supplied information. Measurement verification, BOQ preparation, estimation, and development of unit rates are outside the standard packages.

5. Delivery clock and incomplete inputs

The delivery period starts only after the required information is complete and accepted, scope is confirmed, and any agreed payment condition is met. Business days and the start date should be identified in the written agreement. Missing, conflicting, oversized, or revised inputs may require clarification, an adjusted deadline, or a revised quote. Any pause and its impact will be communicated; missing documents do not automatically remove cancellation or refund rights.

6. Revisions and changes

Included revisions address feedback within the original agreed scope. New work areas, additional activities, changed budgets, new reporting periods, or substantial changes to the underlying project are scope changes. Price and timing for additional work must be agreed before it proceeds. The agreement should state a reasonable review period and the process for reporting an incomplete or defective deliverable.

7. Deliverables and use

The written agreement should confirm the file formats, software-version requirements, and the client’s permitted use of the delivered work, including sharing with its project stakeholders. Client-supplied materials remain the client’s or the relevant rights holder’s property. Ownership and licensing of pre-existing tools, templates, and project deliverables must be agreed expressly; this draft does not create an intellectual-property transfer.

A schedule is a planning and reporting tool based on the inputs and assumptions supplied. It is not a guarantee of project completion, recovery of delay, contractual entitlement, third-party approval, or a particular commercial result. Expert delay analysis, EOT claims, and contract-specific opinions require separately agreed specialist scope.

8. Confidentiality and document exchange

Confidentiality obligations, authorized access, storage arrangements, and retention should be agreed before project documents are transferred. Project documents must be exchanged through the secured portal when enabled, not through email. Files become inaccessible after 12 hours from the upload session creation. Periodic cleanup may remove them later; incidents can delay physical removal. Transaction records are retained separately. Personal information in business communications is addressed by the Privacy Policy.

9. Payment, cancellation, and applicable law

The written quotation should identify the price, any applicable tax, payment timing, and agreed cancellation terms. No recurring planning-service subscription is created by a single schedule-update enquiry. See the proposed Cancellation & Refund Policy for the discussion framework.

Governing law, dispute procedures, and any proportionate limitations of liability require express agreement and legal review. No universal jurisdiction, absolute liability exclusion, or waiver of mandatory consumer rights is imposed by this draft.

10. Portal scope and retention — review draft

Self-service activity extensions are rounded up to blocks of 50. Basic baseline packages allow up to two blocks; Detailed and Update packages allow up to four. Audit activity extensions require a quotation. Without Resources packages exclude material loading. Material add-ons cover at most 50 client-supplied BOQ rows per category, not 50 physical units. Update material assignments require operator review; initial MH/cost setup and recovery services are quote-only.

The proposed operating calendar is Monday through Friday in UTC, with exclusions and holidays identified in the written scope before acceptance. This calendar and the commercial terms require owner and qualified legal review before live sales. Package estimates range from four to twelve business days after complete, usable inputs and scope confirmation. No clock starts solely because a payment succeeds.

Secure uploads are temporary intake, not long-term project storage. Files become inaccessible at the absolute 12-hour deadline. Operators must retrieve needed inputs before that deadline into an approved, access-controlled project system; that subsequent retention and final deliverable transfer procedure require an agreed policy. A verified paid order may request an operator-authorized re-upload without a second charge.
